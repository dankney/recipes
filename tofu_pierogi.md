---
title: Potato & Cheese Pierogi
description: Pan-fried pierogi with a chewy silken tofu dough, shredded Yukon Gold and cheddar filling, and caramelized onions.
---

**Tags:** vegetarian, egg-free

Pierogi with an unconventional dough: blended silken tofu stands in for the usual egg and sour cream, giving a soft dough that comes together in a stand mixer and fries up crisp outside and chewy within. The filling uses shredded Yukon Gold rather than mashed, cooked in butter until browned so it keeps some texture. They go straight into the pan and come out crisp on both faces, finished with a pile of caramelized onions. Makes about 24.

## Ingredients

### Dough

- 10.8 oz silken tofu (one standard package), drained
- 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- 1 teaspoon fine salt

### Caramelized onions

- 1 small yellow onion, sliced thin pole to pole
- 1 tablespoon butter
- pinch of salt

### Filling

- 7 oz Yukon Gold potatoes (about 1 1/2 medium), shredded and wrung dry
- 1/2 small yellow onion, finely diced
- 1 tablespoon butter
- 1/2 cup sharp cheddar, grated (2 oz total, divided — about 1 1/2 oz for the filling, 1/2 oz for topping the rounds)
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- black pepper to taste

### To cook and serve

- 3 tablespoons neutral oil
- 1/2 cup sour cream

## Caramelized Onions

Start these first — they take longer than anything else, and the dough rests while they cook.

1. Melt the butter in a skillet over low heat. Add the sliced onion and a pinch of salt.
2. Cook 25–35 minutes, stirring every few minutes, until deep golden and jammy. Splash in a little water if the fond starts to catch.
3. Set aside. Don't rush this with higher heat — you'll get browned edges and a sharp bite instead of sweetness.

Caramelized onions keep about a week refrigerated, so it's worth making a double batch while you're standing there.

## Dough

1. Put the silken tofu and salt in the stand mixer bowl. With the paddle attachment, beat on medium for 2 minutes, scraping down the sides twice, until completely smooth and creamy with no visible curds.
2. Add the flour a cup at a time on low speed, letting each addition incorporate before the next.
3. Switch to the dough hook and knead on low (speed 2) for 4–5 minutes, until the dough clears the sides of the bowl and forms a smooth ball.
4. Turn out, wrap in plastic, and rest at room temperature at least 30 minutes.

Any curd left in the tofu becomes a weak spot that tears when you roll, so don't shortcut step 1 — the paddle does this better than a hook will.

Hold back flour. Start with 2 1/2 cups and add the last 1/2 cup a few tablespoons at a time — silken tofu brands vary in water content. You want the texture of soft playdough; a stiff dough is much harder to fix than a sticky one. Stop kneading once it's smooth. Unlike bread dough, this one gets tough rather than better with extra time under the hook.

## Filling

1. Wipe out the onion pan. Melt the butter over medium heat, add the diced onion, and cook 5–6 minutes until soft and golden.
2. Add the shredded potato, spread flat, and cook 12–15 minutes, flipping occasionally, until fully tender and browned in spots. Taste a strand before pulling it off the heat — it shouldn't squeak.
3. Off the heat, season with salt and pepper and mash about half the potatoes with a fork so the mixture holds together.
4. Cool to just warm, then fold in about 1 1/2 oz of the cheddar, holding back the rest. Cool completely before filling — warm filling releases moisture that works the seams open.

Run a knife through the shreds a few times before cooking so none are longer than an inch. At this size, long strands make the rounds hard to fold cleanly.

Shred the potatoes into a bowl of cold water, swish to rinse off the surface starch, then drain and wring dry in a towel. The rinse keeps them from graying, and dry shreds brown properly instead of going limp. Do this right before cooking — cut potato oxidizes fast.

Reserve the last 1/2 oz of cheddar for assembly. A pinch laid over the potato in each round melts into a pocket right under the seam, which reads as more cheese than the same amount stirred through the filling.

## Assembly

1. Roll the dough on a well-floured surface to about 1/4 inch. This is thicker than classic pierogi dough and it's deliberate — the tofu wrapper needs the body, and it's where the chew comes from.
2. Cut 3 inch rounds with a cookie cutter, re-rolling the scraps once. You should get about 24.
3. Put a heaping teaspoon of filling on each round and add a pinch of the reserved cheddar on top. Dab the edge with water, fold into a half-moon, and press firmly from the center outward to push out air.
4. Pinch the seam a second time or crimp with a fork. Hold the finished pierogi on a floured tray under a towel.

Overfilling is the main failure mode at this size. Keep the mound compact and centered — you need a clear half-inch of bare dough around it for the seam to hold in the pan. A little filling left over at the end is normal — it keeps a couple of days and is good on toast or folded into eggs.

## Cooking

1. Heat the oil in a large nonstick or cast iron skillet over medium. Arrange the pierogi in a single layer, not touching.
2. Fry 3–4 minutes per side, until both faces are deep golden and crisp. Keep the heat at a steady medium — too hot and the crust sets before the dough cooks through.
3. Work in batches rather than crowding the pan, adding a little more oil between rounds if it runs dry.

Reheat the caramelized onions briefly, pile them over the pierogi, and serve straight from the pan with sour cream.

Uncooked pierogi freeze well on a tray, then bagged. Cook from frozen over slightly lower heat, adding a couple of minutes per side.

## Allergens

- **Soy** — silken tofu.
- **Gluten / wheat** — all-purpose flour. There's no straightforward gluten-free substitution here; the dough needs gluten to hold up to rolling and folding.
- **Milk (dairy)** — butter, cheddar, and sour cream.

This dish is vegetarian, egg-free, and nut-free. Note that traditional cheddar is often made with animal rennet, so use a rennet-free cheese if that matters to you. To make it vegan, swap the butter for oil and use vegan cheddar and sour cream — the dough itself is already dairy- and egg-free.

**Liver-friendly note:** High in sodium (over 1,100 mg a serving) and pan-fried in oil, with saturated fat from cheddar, butter, and sour cream. To make it friendlier: cut the dough salt to 1/2 teaspoon (already suggested), boil rather than pan-fry, use a light sour cream, and go easy on the cheese.
