title: Sandwich Rolls
description: Rustic rolls with a one-hour rise and no kneading or shaping.

**Tags:** vegetarian

A very wet, no-knead dough that goes from bowl to oven in under two hours. There's no shaping — you just dump the risen dough onto a floured counter and cut it into four squares. The dough is too slack to knead, so a short series of folds in the bowl does the work instead.

Makes 4 rolls.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups all-purpose flour, plus extra for the counter
- 1 teaspoon instant yeast
- 1 teaspoon honey
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- 1 cup warm water
- 1 1/2 teaspoons olive oil

## Instructions

1. Stir the honey into the warm water until it dissolves. In a large bowl, combine the flour, yeast, and salt. Add the honey water and olive oil, and mix with a spatula until no dry flour remains.
2. With wet hands, pull one edge of the dough up and fold it over onto itself. Turn the bowl 45 degrees and repeat, for eight folds total.
3. Cover the bowl with plastic wrap and set it in a warm spot to rise for 1 hour.
4. Heavily flour the counter and turn the risen dough out onto it. Flour the top, then cut it into four roughly equal pieces with a knife or bench scraper. Move the pieces to a parchment-lined baking sheet.
5. Bake at 415F for 20–25 minutes in a convection oven, until golden brown. They're small, so start checking at 20 minutes.

## Allergens

- **Gluten / wheat** — all-purpose flour.

These rolls are vegetarian. The honey means they are not vegan; use 1/2 teaspoon of sugar instead for a vegan version, with no change to the rise or flavor. They are free of egg, dairy, nuts, soy, shellfish, and fish.

**Nutritional note:** Fairly liver-friendly — very low in saturated fat, and the only added fat is a teaspoon and a half of olive oil. The main concern is the 1/2 teaspoon of salt (about 290 mg sodium per roll). Cutting it to 1/4 teaspoon brings a roll to roughly 150 mg with no real effect on the rise, though the rolls start to taste flat below that. These are refined-flour rolls, so swapping up to half a cup of the flour for whole wheat adds fiber.
