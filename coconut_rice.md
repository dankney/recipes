**Tags:** vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free

Fluffy jasmine rice with a subtle richness and aroma from cooking it in coconut milk. It's made exactly like plain jasmine rice in a rice cooker, except that part of the water is swapped for coconut milk — a volume equal to the dry rice.

Makes 4 servings (about 3/4 cup cooked each, ~3 cups total).

## Ingredients

- 1 cup dry jasmine rice
- 1 cup full-fat coconut milk
- 1/2 cup water
- 1/4 teaspoon salt (optional)

The coconut milk matches the dry rice by volume (1 cup each). Together with the 1/2 cup water, that's 1.5 cups of total liquid — the standard jasmine rice-cooker ratio of 1 part rice to 1.5 parts liquid. If your rice cooker uses a different ratio for jasmine rice, keep the coconut milk at 1 cup and adjust only the water.

## Instructions

1. Rinse the rice: cover it with cool water, swish, and drain. Repeat 2–3 times until the water runs mostly clear, then drain well.
2. Add the rinsed rice to the rice cooker.
3. Shake or stir the can of coconut milk so the cream and liquid are combined, then add 1 cup coconut milk, 1/2 cup water, and the salt.
4. Cook on the rice cooker's white/jasmine setting.
5. When it finishes, let it rest for a few minutes, then fluff with a fork or rice paddle and serve.

**To scale:** use equal parts rice and coconut milk, plus half that amount in water (e.g. 2 cups rice → 2 cups coconut milk + 1 cup water for ~8 servings).

## Allergens

- **Tree nut (coconut)** — the coconut milk. The FDA classifies coconut as a tree nut, so it's relevant for tree-nut allergies (though many people with tree-nut allergies tolerate coconut — check with your allergist).

This dish is vegan and gluten-free. It is otherwise free of dairy, egg, soy, peanuts, wheat, shellfish, and fish. (Some canned coconut milk contains stabilizers like guar gum; check the label if that matters to you.)

**Liver-friendly note:** Full-fat coconut milk is high in saturated fat. Use light coconut milk (about half the saturated fat and calories) or replace part of the coconut milk with water or broth. Sodium is already low if you skip the optional salt.
